### Essential Question

How can you compare fractions with unlike numerators and denominators?

### Visual Learning Bridge

Ibrahim’s father is building a model dinosaur. Compare ¼ inch and ⅗ inch. Then compare ½ inch and ⁵⁄₁₀ inch.

You can compare these fractions because they refer to the same whole, an inch.

The fraction that is **farther to the right** on a number line is greater.

**Compare ¼ and ⅗** by renaming so they both have the same denominator.

```
1/4 = (1 × 3)/(4 × 3) = 3/12
3/5 = (3 × 2)? wait: 4 and 5 → LCD 20, or 12 as in the book:
Book uses:
1/4 = 3/12,  5/6 related example.

From the dump:
1 × 3 / 4 × 3 = 3/12
5 × 2 / 6 × 2 = 10/12
3/12 < 10/12 so 1/4 < 5/6
```

When two fractions have the **same numerators**, the fraction with the **smaller denominator** is greater (larger pieces).

**Convince Me!** Critique Reasoning: Khalil said, “These are easy to compare. I just think about ½.” Circle the greater of ⅝ and ⁵⁄₈-related fractions. Explain what Khalil was thinking.

### Another Example

Compare ⅗ and ⁶⁄₁₀. Create an equivalent so numerators are the same: ⁶⁄₁₀ ÷ 2/2 = ⅗. Equal.

When two fractions have the same numerators, the fraction with the smaller denominator is greater: ¾ > ⅗ because fourths are larger pieces than fifths.
